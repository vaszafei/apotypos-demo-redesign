The case of Marseille

On 17 October, a CNews journalist said that rioters in one Marseille street burned only the Jewish-owned shops. The Jewish community security service (SPCJ) said that it had no evidence that the rioters targeted Jewish businesses on purpose. CRIF Marseille said that Jews did not appear to be singled out. This section shows how the claim occurs in the 998 posts.

What the targeted searches found

Facebook search gave travel posts about Marseille for most results of "Marseille Jewish shops". These posts are out of scope.

Limitations

What this sample can and cannot show

  • The posts are Facebook search results for one account. Facebook ranks the results for that account. The sample is not a census of Facebook.
  • The search terms control the mix. A search that names a group finds more posts about that group. Use the neutral-search bars for a less biased share.
  • One coder (a language model) gave each post one cluster from the first 230 characters. A second person did not check the codes.
  • The page does not separate pages from personal profiles, because the poster names are stored as hashes only.
  • "Same poster, 2+ posts" counts posters that occur in two or more searches. News pages occur often, so this signal is weak on its own.
  • The reach signal uses the Facebook "thousand" mark (χιλ.) in the visible counts. It does not separate reactions, comments and shares.
  • 65 posts are out of scope: posts from before October 2026 or posts about other events.
