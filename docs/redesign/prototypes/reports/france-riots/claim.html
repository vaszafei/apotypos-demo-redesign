"Only Jewish shops were burned in Marseille": where the claim came from and who carried it

On 17 October a French TV journalist said rioters in one Marseille street burned every Jewish-owned shop and spared the Muslim-owned ones. This page follows that claim from French TV to X, the Greek and Israeli press and Facebook, and sets out what the sources say about it.

Verdict as of 20 October

Jewish-owned shops were damaged. The claim that only they were targeted is unsupported.

Confirmed

Damage happened

  • Jewish-owned shops in Marseille were set on fire or damaged during the 17–18 October riots, along with many others (JTA).
  • A temporary synagogue in Paris's 19th arrondissement was broken into. A committee member said it "primarily points to an attempted theft" (JTA).
Not supported

Selective targeting

  • SPCJ, the Jewish community's security service, said there was "no evidence that Jewish institutions or businesses were deliberately targeted" (JTA).
  • CRIF Marseille's president said Jews "did not appear to have been singled out" (JNS). Per Snopes, he also said all shops were hit, "Jewish, non-Jewish or Muslim".
  • Per Snopes, Marseille police said the looting had "no connection" to anti-Jewish sentiment.
Origin

One unsourced TV remark

  • Paul Amar on CNews, 17 October. He gave no source for the address lists or the street (Proto Thema, Snopes).
  • No French fact-checker (AFP Factuel, CheckNews, Les Décodeurs) had published on it by 20 October.
  • Snopes is paywalled; its statements here come from search summaries, not the full text.

    On Facebook the claim is rare, and so is the backlash

    933 posts in scope (of 998) from 37 Facebook searches on the riots in English, Greek and French, and comments from 9 posts about the claim. The counts use the same rules as the Facebook tab.

    One public counter-post, by Greek journalist Aris Chatzistefanou on 20 October, calls the claim "Israeli fake news". It links the JPost op-ed above, which did repeat the claim, while JPost's and JNS's news reports did not.

    Limitations

    What this tracker can and cannot show

    • The Greek and Israeli corpora are what Apotypos collected for 1–20 October. Outlets missing from them (News247, Israel Hayom, i24, Kan) may have carried the claim.
    • Facebook results are search results ranked for one account. They are not a census of Facebook. The comment sample (52 comments, 50 commenters, 7 with Greek-script names; one commenter, not Greek, appears on two posts) is too small to show or rule out coordination.
    • US partisan sites (PJ Media, Townhall) appear only in web search results, not in an Apotypos corpus. X posts are cited from search results and from the tweet embedded in Newsit's article.
    • The verdict reflects statements published by 20 October. Police investigations in Paris and Marseille were ongoing.
