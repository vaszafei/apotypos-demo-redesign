France Riots in French Media
Apotypos · French press · France, 1–20 Oct 2026

How the French press framed its own riots

This page shows how 11 French outlets reported the school protests and the riots of 17 October 2026, from 1 to 20 October. It uses the same method as the Greek press tab: each headline has one frame, and word measures count the words that outlets use. It also shows who owns each outlet.

How the corpus was made
Headline frames

Frames are coded from the headline. One frame for each headline. The colour shows the family: grey for reporting, red for "against the protesters", green for "with the protesters", yellow for political fallout.

Stance by owner

Frames are coded from the headline. Share of each owner group's headlines framed against the protesters (protester violence, far left, migration or Islam) and with them (police violence, causes, solidarity). The rest is reporting, politics and other news. Owner groups with fewer than 50 articles show counts, not percentages.

Every outlet at a glance

Frame families and words, by outlet

Frame columns: Frames are coded from the headline. Word columns are text mentions: words are counted in the headline and the first 1,200 characters of the text. Cells give the percent of each outlet's articles, or a count for outlets with fewer than 50 articles.

Two phases

Articles for each day, coloured by frame family. Frames are coded from the headline.

Migration, Islam and Jewish shops

Frames are coded from the headline. Every headline coded "migration / Islam blamed" or "Jewish shops / antisemitism". The Narrative tab gives the voice of each one and the timeline across countries.

Copy-paste echoes

5-word shingles, overlap of 50% or more, on the first 3,000 characters of the text. Groups with the most outlets.

Limitations

What this corpus can and cannot show

  • Libération refused all 297 requests, and Le Parisien most of them. So the left-leaning daily Libération is absent, and Le Parisien has only 15 articles.
  • CNews pages give only 60–90 words of text, so the word measures under-count CNews. Its headlines are complete.
  • Europe 1 publishes many short radio clips, one for each guest. So its count is high, and its frames show the guests that it invites.
  • One coder (a language model) gave each headline one frame. A second person did not check the codes. A headline is not the whole article.
  • The text that the crawler keeps can include sidebars added later. The word measures use the lead only for this reason.
  • Owners are as of 2025. "Bolloré" means the outlets controlled by the Bolloré family through Vivendi, Canal+ and Lagardère: Europe 1, the JDD and CNews.
