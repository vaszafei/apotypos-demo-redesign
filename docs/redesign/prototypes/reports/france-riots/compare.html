Facebook vs the Greek press: which story each tells about the French riots

The share of each narrative in 933 public Facebook posts and in 976 articles from 28 Greek outlets, both about the October 2026 unrest in France. Both datasets were coded one item, one narrative. Here both codings are mapped onto one shared list, so the shares can sit side by side.

Read this before the numbers The units differ. A Facebook post was coded from the preview text shown before "See more", and a press article from its headline. The Facebook sample comes from Facebook search results ranked for one account, and the press corpus from what 28 outlets published on 1–19 October. One AI model (Claude) coded both, with no second coder. Read the gaps as direction and size, not exact decimals. The mapping between the two codings is in the table at the end. The headline numbers use the 664 posts from neutral searches only (searches that do not name a group or a side). The codebooks are not symmetric: of Facebook posts are in narratives that the press codebook does not have ("Facebook only").

Limitations

What this comparison can and cannot show

  • Facebook posts and press articles are different units: a post is often one line of opinion, an article is a report with a headline. Facebook preview text and press headlines are both short, which is why they were used, but they are not the same thing.
  • "Violence and looting condemned" joins Facebook posts of grief and condemnation with press headlines that lead on protester violence. The two are close but not identical.
  • Some narratives exist in one dataset only. No press headline was coded "viral spectacle", "media cover-up" or "Jewish safety", and no Facebook post was coded "police violence". Sensational wording in the press is measured separately on the press dashboard (19% of headlines).
  • The Facebook sample has 933 posts in scope from 37 searches. Some searches name a group, for example "France riots Islam". These searches increase the share of that group. So the headline numbers on this tab use the 664 posts from neutral searches. The charts show both.
  • The two codebooks are not symmetric. Facebook has six narratives that the press codebook does not have (viral spectacle, other, pushback, Jewish safety, media cover-up, Israel blamed). They hold of the posts. Only one narrative, police violence, is press only.
  • Two kinds of measure are on these tabs. Headline frames give one code to each headline. Text mentions count words in the title and the body. This tab uses headline frames (press) and preview-text clusters (Facebook) only.
